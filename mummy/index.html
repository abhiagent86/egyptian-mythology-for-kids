Make a Mummy

The Egyptians believed a person needed their body in the next life. So they learned how to make a body last for thousands of years. Can you put the six steps in the right order?

What comes first? A king has died. Help the priests turn him into a mummy. Tap the six steps in the right order.

Stuck? Here are all six steps

Show the steps in order
  1. Wash the body. The priests wash the body with water from the river Nile and sweet-smelling palm wine.
  2. Take out the insides. The soft parts inside are taken out so the body will not rot. The lungs, liver and stomach go into four special jars. The heart stays in, because Egyptians believed you think with your heart. And the brain? It was pulled out through the nose with a hook!
  3. Cover with salt. The body is covered with a special salt called natron for 40 days. The salt soaks up all the water, so the body dries out completely.
  4. Rub with oils. The dry body is rubbed with oils to make the skin soft again. It is filled with linen or sawdust so it gets its shape back.
  5. Wrap in linen. The body is wrapped in hundreds of metres of linen strips. Lucky charms called amulets are tucked between the layers to keep the person safe.
  6. Add a mask and a coffin. A mask goes over the face, so the spirit can find its own body again. Then the mummy is laid in a painted coffin and carried to its tomb.

Four mummy facts

  • Making a mummy took about 70 days from start to finish.
  • Anubis, the god with a jackal head, looked after mummies. The chief priest sometimes wore an Anubis mask.
  • The Egyptians made millions of animal mummies: cats, birds called ibises, falcons, and even crocodiles.
  • You can see the real mummies of kings and queens in the Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo.

For grown-ups: This is a simplified version of the most careful, expensive method, as described for royal burials. Cheaper methods skipped steps, and practice changed over 3,000 years. The game mentions that the brain was removed through the nose; nothing is shown.
