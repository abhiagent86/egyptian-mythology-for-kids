Ra

say: RAH

God of the sun

Name in hieroglyphs
𓂋𓂝𓇳The Egyptians said Ra (also written Re).
What the name means
"Sun". His name is simply the Egyptian word for the sun.
How to spot Ra
A falcon head with a big red sun disc on top.
Famous for
Sailing the sun across the sky in a boat every day, and bringing light to the world.
Main temple town
Heliopolis, the "city of the sun", near modern Cairo

Nut

say: NOOT

Goddess of the sky

Name in hieroglyphs
𓏌𓏏𓇯The Egyptians said Nut.
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure. Her name ends with the sign for "sky".
How to spot Nut
A woman covered in stars, bending over the earth like a bridge.
Famous for
Being the sky itself. She is the mother of Osiris, Isis, Set and Nephthys.
Main temple town
None of her own. Look for her painted on the ceilings of tombs and temples.

Geb

say: GEB

God of the earth

Name in hieroglyphs
𓅬𓃀𓀭The Egyptians said Geb.
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure. His name is written with a goose.
How to spot Geb
A man lying on the ground under the sky, often with green skin or a goose on his head.
Famous for
Being the earth itself. People said earthquakes were Geb laughing.
Main temple town
None of his own

Osiris

say: oh-SY-ris

King of the afterlife

Name in hieroglyphs
𓊨𓁹𓀭The Egyptians said Usir.
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure! "Osiris" is how the Greeks said his name.
How to spot Osiris
Green skin, a tall white crown with two feathers, and a crook and flail in his hands.
Famous for
Being a kind king who taught people to farm. He later became king of the land of spirits.
Main temple town
Abydos

Isis

say: EYE-sis

Goddess of magic and love

Name in hieroglyphs
𓊨𓏏𓆇𓁐The Egyptians said Aset.
What the name means
"Throne". That is why she wears a throne on her head.
How to spot Isis
A little throne on her head. Sometimes she has big bird wings.
Famous for
Strong magic, and never giving up when her family needed her. She is the mother of Horus.
Main temple town
Philae, an island temple in the south of Egypt

Set

say: SET

God of storms and the desert

Name in hieroglyphs
𓃩The Egyptians said Setekh (or Sutekh).
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure.
How to spot Set
A mystery animal that nobody can name: a long curved snout and tall square ears.
Famous for
Causing trouble for Osiris and Horus. But he is also strong and brave: he guards Ra's boat from the giant snake at night.
Main temple town
Ombos (Naqada)

Horus

say: HOR-us

God of the sky and of kings

Name in hieroglyphs
𓅃The Egyptians said Heru.
What the name means
"The far-away one" or "the one high above", like a falcon in the sky.
How to spot Horus
A falcon head wearing the red and white double crown of Egypt.
Famous for
Becoming king of Egypt after many contests with his uncle Set. His healed eye is a sign of safety.
Main temple town
Edfu, one of the best-kept temples in Egypt

Thoth

say: THOTH

God of writing and clever ideas

Name in hieroglyphs
𓅝The Egyptians said Djehuty.
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure. "Thoth" is how the Greeks said his name.
How to spot Thoth
The head of an ibis, a bird with a long curved beak. Sometimes he is a baboon.
Famous for
Inventing writing, solving problems with clever ideas, and keeping the records of the gods.
Main temple town
Hermopolis

Anubis

say: ah-NOO-bis

Guide to the afterlife

Name in hieroglyphs
𓇋𓈖𓊪𓅱𓃢The Egyptians said Anpu.
What the name means
Nobody knows for sure. "Anubis" is how the Greeks said his name.
How to spot Anubis
The head of a black jackal, a wild dog with tall pointed ears.
Famous for
Looking after mummies, guiding people to Osiris, and weighing hearts against the feather of truth.
Main temple town
Cynopolis, which means "city of the dog"

Ma'at

say: mah-AHT

Goddess of truth

Name in hieroglyphs
𓌷𓂝𓏏𓁦The Egyptians said Maat.
What the name means
"Truth" and "what is right".
How to spot Ma'at
A tall ostrich feather on her head.
Famous for
Truth and fairness. Her feather is the one that hearts are weighed against.
Main temple town
A small temple at Karnak

For grown-ups: Hieroglyphs are shown in a simple row, in one common spelling; scribes arranged them in blocks and used several spellings. Egyptian writing left out vowels, so "Usir", "Aset", "Heru" and the like are the way Egyptologists say the names today, not a record of how they sounded.

Many gods changed over 3,000 years. Isis, for example, was later often shown with cow horns and a sun disc. The pictures here use the clearest marker for each god.
