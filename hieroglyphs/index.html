Five facts about hieroglyphs

  • There were more than 700 different hieroglyphs. Some stand for a sound and some for a whole word.
  • You can read them from left to right or from right to left. The animals and people always look towards the start of the line.
  • Egyptians did not write most vowel sounds, such as A, E and O. So nobody knows exactly how their words sounded.
  • Only a few people could read and write. They were called scribes and went to school for many years.
  • For 1,400 years nobody could read hieroglyphs. Then the Rosetta Stone helped Champollion work them out in 1822.

For grown-ups: The converter matches each English letter to the closest Egyptian one-sound sign, one letter at a time. It is the usual playful "alphabet", not how a scribe would have spelled a name. The signs for E, O and L are stand-ins: scribes used them that way only in later times, to spell foreign names such as Cleopatra.
